Childhood immunization · 2000–2025

Twenty-five years of new vaccines

Four vaccines that were rare outside rich countries in 2000 now reach most of the world’s infants. What changed, where the work is left, and what came with it.

The core vaccines were widespread — 72% of infants got all three DTP doses — but the newer ones reached few children outside Europe and the Americas.

By 2025, they reached most of them

103M

Country by country, the map rose as national programmes added the newer vaccines. The biggest gains were in the most populous countries that started from almost nothing: India, Nigeria, Pakistan.

The work left

Twenty million infants still miss out

20.3M
infants did not get the third DTP dose in 2025

37% of them live in five countries: Nigeria, DR Congo, India, Pakistan and Yemen. Where routine DTP doesn’t reach, the newer vaccines don’t either.

Missed infants = births × the share without the third dose. Births are UN estimates and projections.

Meanwhile

Child mortality halved

7.7% → 3.7%
of children died before age five, 2000 and 2024

Had 2000’s rate held, about 5.2 million more children would have died in 2024 alone.

Vaccines are one of several reasons, alongside malaria control, nutrition, clean water and care at birth; this data shows the change, not each cause’s share of it.

Sources and method

Where these numbers come from

    “Newer vaccines” are hepatitis B (third dose), Hib (third dose), pneumococcal conjugate (completed series) and rotavirus (completed series). A country’s reach is the mean of the four; a vaccine not yet in its schedule counts as zero. Every number in this deck is computed from these datasets by the visualization.
