The Height Limit of Trees

The tallest living things on Earth all stop at about the same height. The reason isn’t wood or wind. It’s water.

Specimens 01–09Tallest of each species

Nine giants, one ceiling

The tallest measured tree of each of the world’s nine tallest species, drawn side by side at true scale against a board ruled every ten metres. For scale: the Saturn V rocket, London’s Elizabeth Tower and the Statue of Liberty.

They evolved on four continents — redwoods and spruces, a Himalayan cypress, Tasmanian eucalypts, rainforest giants from Borneo and the Amazon — yet all stop between 88 and 117 metres.

Lifting water a hundred metres

Every litre of water Hyperion’s top leaves use is pulled up from the soil through the wood. Nothing pumps it: water evaporating from the leaves tugs on the column below, held together by the cohesion of water itself.

Each ring on the trunk marks ten metres. The higher the leaf, the harder the pull has to be.

The predictionKoch et al., 2004

A ceiling at 122–130 metres

Climbing tall redwoods with instruments, Koch and colleagues measured how water stress rises with height: gravity alone costs about 0.01 megapascals of suction for every metre. Near the top, leaves grow smaller and photosynthesize less.

Extrapolating, they predicted no redwood can grow beyond 122–130 metres — the hatched band. The tallest trees on Earth stop just beneath it.

About this exhibit

  1. Heights: the tallest reliably measured living specimen of each species, as compiled in Wikipedia’s List of superlative trees from laser and tape-drop measurements (Native Tree Society, Gymnosperm Database, Ren et al. 2024), retrieved November 2026. Crown and bark are drawn to read as each species; only heights are measured.
  2. The limit and the water-stress gradient: Koch, Sillett, Jennings & Davis, “The limits to tree height,” Nature 428 (2004).
  3. The hands-on model is a simplification for teaching: suction lost per metre to gravity (0.0098 MPa × g) and to wood friction (0.004 MPa, assumed), from soil water at −0.3 MPa down to a working leaf limit of −2.0 MPa (assumed). With these values it reproduces the paper’s prediction on Earth; it is not a forecast for other worlds.
  4. Landmarks: Saturn V 110.6 m; Elizabeth Tower 96 m; Statue of Liberty 93 m, ground to torch.
