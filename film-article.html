The business of the box office

The 2.5× Rule

A blockbuster has to earn about two and a half times what it cost just to break even in theaters. Hollywood’s biggest bets are the ones that most often fall short.

45 wide releases · 1997–2024

When a studio announces that a film cost $250 million, that number is only the production budget. It leaves out the global marketing campaign, which can add another 50 to 100 percent, and it hides the fact that cinemas keep roughly half of every ticket sold. Put those together and a rough rule falls out: a film needs to gross about 2.5 times its budget worldwide before the theatrical run pays for itself.

The rule is crude. Streaming, television and merchandise money arrive later and can rescue a film. But it is the line studios watch on opening weekend, and it explains why the most expensive films are often the riskiest.

Scene one

Above the line, and below it

Each light is a film, placed by what it cost and what it grossed worldwide. The thin bright line is the 2.5× threshold. Films above it, in amber, earned their money back in theaters; films below it, in red, did not.

Most clear the line comfortably. The failures gather in one corner: the far right, where budgets pass $200 million.

Scene two

Where tentpoles fall short

Closer in, on films that cost $100 million or more. Each red line runs from what a film grossed up to what it needed.

The Marvels cost a reported $274 million and grossed $206 million, leaving it $479M short. Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny took in $384 million and still came up $354M short.

Scene three

Small films, huge multiples

Rank every film by how many times over it earned its budget and the order flips. A $4.5 million horror film tops the bill: Get Out returned 57× its cost. Parasite returned 23×.

A cheap film doesn’t need a huge audience to look like a triumph. A $300 million film needs a historic one just to break even.

Scene four

The critics don’t decide it

Line the films up by their reviews and the money barely follows. Transformers: Age of Extinction scored 18% on Rotten Tomatoes and grossed $1.1 billion.

Killers of the Flower Moon, at 93%, lost money in theaters. Acclaim alone can’t carry a $200 million budget.

Scene five

Two kinds of studio bet

Sorted by genre, two strategies separate. Horror is cheap and nearly always profitable: a median budget of $17 million and a median return of 20×.

Action and sci-fi spend ten times as much for a fraction of the multiple. They still bring in most of the money, and they carry most of the risk.
